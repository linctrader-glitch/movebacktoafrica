Moving Back to Africa in 2026: Your Most-Asked Questions, Answered

By Ada Eguzouwa. Updated November 2026.

I looked at what people are searching for and asking right now about moving back to Africa, then checked the latest news on visas, citizenship, money and banking. Here are straight answers to the 12 questions that come up most.

1. Can I just move to Africa?

You can visit most African countries easily. Living there long-term is different: you need a legal right to stay, such as citizenship, a residence permit, a work permit or a special diaspora status like Ghana's Right of Abode. If you were born in the country or have a parent from there, you may already be a citizen. Start by checking that.

The answer also depends on your passport. An American, British or Canadian passport gets you a tourist visa, not the right to live and work. Plan which visa or permit you'll actually live on before you sell anything.

2. Do I need a visa?

It depends on which passport you travel on, and 2025–2026 brought big changes for African passport holders:

  • Ghana started a free visa regime for all African passport holders on 25 May 2026. You still apply online for an e-visa, but you pay no fee.
  • Kenya dropped its electronic travel authorisation (eTA) for most African countries in July 2025. Most African visitors can now stay up to two months with no visa or fee. Somalia and Libya were left out.
  • Benin, Rwanda, The Gambia and Seychelles already let all African passport holders in without a visa.

If you travel on a US, UK, Canadian or European passport, these changes don't apply to you, and you'll usually need a visa or eTA. And remember: visa-free entry is for visits. It doesn't give you the right to live or work there.

3. Can I still get Ghanaian citizenship in 2026?

Not right now through the diaspora route. In February 2026, Ghana's Ministry of the Interior and the Diaspora Affairs Office paused citizenship applications from the historic diaspora to make the process simpler and less costly. They promised updated guidelines "in due course" but gave no date. When I checked in November 2026, I couldn't find an official notice that applications had reopened.

The Right of Abode is a separate status. It lets people of African descent live in Ghana permanently, enter without a visa and work without a work permit. Check its current status with the Ghana Immigration Service before you plan around either route.

Watch out for "agents". When a government pauses a process, people appear offering to "fast-track" it. Only trust official government channels.

4. Which countries offer citizenship to the diaspora?

Four West African countries have special citizenship routes for the historic African diaspora: Ghana, Sierra Leone, Benin and Guinea-Bissau. The numbers are still small: the Migration Policy Institute counts about 1,213 people granted citizenship through these programmes since 2016, most of them in Ghana. Sierra Leone accepts proof of ancestry including DNA tests, and Benin passed its law in 2024.

If you were born in an African country or your parents were, you usually don't need any of these: you may already be a citizen by birth or descent. My guide Where Should I Move in Africa? covers how to compare countries.

5. How much money do I need?

There's no single number. It depends on your city, your family and, most of all, your income. Plan for four "buckets": the move itself, your first 90 days, your monthly living costs, and a safety net of at least 12 months of living costs plus enough to return abroad if you need to.

6. Is it cheaper to live in Africa now?

Some things are, some things aren't, and it's changing fast. Take Nigeria:

  • Food and rent have gone up a lot. In January 2026, food prices were at their highest in over 20 years, and rent was named the biggest cost for most people.
  • The naira has strengthened. It closed June 2026 at about ₦1,376 to the dollar, compared with about ₦1,532 a year earlier. That's good news for the country, but it means your dollars, pounds or Canadian dollars buy fewer naira than they did a year ago.

So if your plan was "my foreign savings will go much further back home", check today's exchange rate and today's prices, not last year's. Your savings may stretch less than you expect.

7. How do I open a bank account back home from abroad?

For Nigerians, this got much easier. Since May 2025, the Non-Resident Bank Verification Number (NRBVN) lets Nigerians living abroad get a BVN online, without flying home. Through it you can open naira and domiciliary accounts (USD, GBP or EUR) with partner banks. It's open to Nigerians in the diaspora and people who are Nigerian by descent. The registration fee was reported at about $50 when it launched. Apply through the official NIBSS platform or your bank's website, never through an unofficial agent.

For other countries, ask the banks you're considering whether you can open an account as a non-resident, and what ID you'll need. Keep at least one account abroad open too, so you can always reach your money and credit.

8. How will I earn a living?

This is the question that decides whether a move lasts. The safest plans have income that keeps coming after you land: a remote job, freelance or consulting clients, a business that already runs, rent, a pension, or digital products you sell online. Test it before you move: work for a few weeks from where you'd live, and check that the internet, power and time zones work for you.

Starting a business back home can work too, but test it first and don't depend on it for your rent in year one.

9. What about health care?

For many people, especially retirees, health care is the biggest worry, even ahead of security. As a nurse, I agree it should be near the top of your list. Before you move:

  • Find the nearest good hospital and visit it.
  • Get health insurance that works there, ideally with emergency evacuation cover.
  • Check that your regular medication is available, genuine and affordable.
  • Know where you'd go in an emergency and how long it takes to get there.

10. Is it safe?

Africa is 54 countries, so this question only has an answer for a specific city and neighbourhood. Read your own government's travel advice for that country, then ask people who live in that exact area. Think about everyday risks too, like road safety, scams and theft. Choose your area carefully, and verify people before you trust them with money.

11. Where should I move?

If you're from a particular country, home is the obvious start, but compare cities, not just countries. If you're part of the historic diaspora, Ghana is still the best-known choice, with Sierra Leone, Benin, Rwanda, Kenya and others also drawing returnees. Compare your options on your legal right to stay, income, cost, safety, health care, schools, language and community. My guide Where Should I Move in Africa? walks you through it.

12. Is moving back a good idea, or will I regret it?

People move back for real reasons: the rising cost of living abroad, wanting to be near family, and wanting more peace of mind. And many people are glad they did. The ones who regret it usually moved without an income plan, budgeted with old prices, or trusted the wrong people with their money.
