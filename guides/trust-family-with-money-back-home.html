Should You Trust Family to Manage Your Money Back Home?

By Ada Eguzouwa. Updated November 2026.

This week, Nation FM in Kenya asked its listeners: "If you were investing back home from abroad, would you trust family to manage your money?" It's one of the most painful questions in the diaspora. Here's my honest answer, and a system that protects both your money and your family relationships.

The short answer

Trust your family with your love. Don't give anyone, family or not, unchecked control of your money.

This isn't about saying your relatives are bad people. Most aren't. The problem is the arrangement: one person, far from you, holding your money, with no paperwork, no checks and a lot of pressure on them. That arrangement goes wrong so often that it has become a running story across the diaspora, from Nairobi to Lagos to Accra.

Why this matters to me. Since moving back to Nigeria, I've built Manna Farms and consulted with members of the diaspora who are investing back home. Again and again, the projects that work are the ones built on systems, not blind trust.

Why it goes wrong, even with good people

  • Nobody wrote anything down. "Just send the money and I'll handle it" leaves no budget, no receipts and no agreement on what success looks like.
  • Pressure and emergencies. Your building money is sitting in a relative's account when school fees, a hospital bill or a funeral comes up. "I'll put it back later" is how many projects stall.
  • Inexperience. Kenya's Daily Nation has pointed out that families don't always lose diaspora money out of greed: some simply aren't used to managing large sums. Building a house or running a farm is a skill.
  • Photos aren't proof. There are stories of relatives sending fake progress photos of buildings that were never built.
  • It's hard to check on family. Asking for receipts from your own brother feels like an insult, so people don't ask, until it's too late.

What it really costs

The stories are painful and common. Kenyan media has reported many cases of people abroad who sent money home through relatives for land or buildings, then came home to find nothing on the ground. One Kenyan diaspora adviser told Kenyans.co.ke that if you put 300 Kenyans in the diaspora in a room, at least half of them would have lost money trying to buy land.

And the loss isn't only money. It's years of overtime abroad, a broken relationship with a sibling or parent, and often the dream of coming home at all.

7 rules if family is involved

You can still involve family. Give them a role that suits them, and keep the money flowing through a system you control.

  1. Family can be your eyes, not your wallet. Let a relative visit the site, introduce you to people and report back. Don't let them hold the project money.
  2. Pay suppliers and workers directly. Pay the cement supplier, the contractor or the landlord yourself, from your own account, with an invoice. In Nigeria, the Non-Resident BVN now lets you open a local account from abroad, which makes this much easier.
  3. Pay in stages, and verify each stage first. Never send the next payment until you've confirmed the last one with receipts and a live video call you control, or a visit from someone independent.
  4. Everything in your name. Land titles, company registration and bank accounts should be in your name, with documents you've checked yourself at the land registry or company registry.
  5. Use at least one independent professional. A lawyer, surveyor or project manager who is not family and reports to you. Check their registration and references.
  6. Write it down and pay them fairly. If a relative is doing real work, agree a role, a budget and a fee in writing. Paid, defined work is respected more than "help".
  7. Go home and look. Budget for regular trips. A flight is cheap compared with losing a whole project because no one checked.

Other ways to invest from abroad

  • Reputable, regulated firms. Advisers who work with the diaspora often say to deal only with reputable firms rather than friends or relatives. Check that any firm is properly registered, and search its name for complaints before you pay.
  • Diaspora products from banks. Many African banks now offer diaspora accounts, mortgages and investment products you can manage online.
  • Professional project managers. For a building project, a paid project manager who sends verified reports can cost far less than one failed stage.
  • Start smaller. Test your system on a small project first. If it works, scale up.

Red flags, even from people you love

  • "Just send the money first. I'll send the documents later."
  • They discourage you from visiting, or from checking the land registry yourself.
  • Progress photos, but never a live video call.
  • The budget keeps growing, but nothing new appears on the ground.

How to say no without breaking the family

The hardest part is the conversation. Make the system the reason, not the person:

"I love you and I want you involved. I've decided to run all my projects the same way: I pay suppliers directly and keep the paperwork in my name. It's not about you, it's how I protect our family's future. Will you be my eyes on the ground?"

Agree the rules before any money moves. It's much easier to set a system at the start than to change one after something has gone wrong.

Common questions

Should I trust family to manage my money back home?
Trust your family with your love, but don't give anyone, family or not, unchecked control of your money. Give relatives a role such as being your eyes on the ground, and keep payments in a system you control: pay suppliers directly, pay in verified stages and keep documents in your name.
Why do diaspora projects managed by family fail?
Usually because of the arrangement, not because relatives are bad people: nothing is written down, emergencies put pressure on the money, the relative may not be experienced at managing large sums, and photos are accepted as proof without independent checks.
How can I invest back home safely from abroad?
Pay suppliers directly from your own account, pay in stages and verify each stage with receipts and a live video call you control, keep land titles and accounts in your name, use at least one independent professional, and visit regularly.
How do I tell my family I don't want them handling my money?
Make the system the reason, not the person. Explain that you run every project the same way, paying suppliers directly and keeping paperwork in your name, and invite them to be your eyes on the ground. Agree the rules before any money moves.
