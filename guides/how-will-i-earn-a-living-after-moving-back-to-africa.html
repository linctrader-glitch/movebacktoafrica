How Will I Earn a Living After I Move Back to Africa?

By Ada Eguzouwa. Updated November 2026.

Money is the number one fear behind every move home. The good news: in 2026 you don't have to choose between earning well and living in Africa. Here are six real ways to earn, how to test them before you move, and the setup you need to get paid from anywhere.

The one rule: income before the ticket

The people who stay happily are usually the ones whose income kept coming after they landed. The people who go back abroad are often the ones who planned to "figure it out when I get there", then watched their savings disappear while they searched.

My rule: have at least one income stream that already works from Africa before you buy a one-way ticket. Savings buy you time. Income buys you a life.

Six ways to earn from Africa

Pick one or two. Don't try all six at once.

1. Keep your job, remotely

The simplest path, if your employer agrees. Ask early, offer a trial period, and agree in writing on hours, time zones and how you'll be paid. Remote and contract roles in IT, finance, admin, customer support, writing and some health roles (like telehealth coordination) can often be done from anywhere with good internet.

2. Digital products

Turn what you already know into ebooks, workbooks, templates, checklists or short courses that sell online, again and again. A nurse can make a caregiver guide; an accountant, a small-business bookkeeping template; a teacher, lesson plans.

My own story. I created my workbook, How to Move Back to Africa, with AI, put it on this website, ran one Facebook ad, and made my first sale the same night. That's the power of a digital product: you build it once and it can sell while you sleep, from anywhere.

3. Digital marketing services

Run Facebook and Instagram ads, manage social media pages or write content for businesses. Businesses across Africa need this, and clients abroad pay in strong currencies.

4. Coaching and consulting in your profession

Your career abroad is an asset. Offer paid calls or programmes: helping others pass licensing exams, advising African startups on HR or finance, or guiding people through the process you've already been through.

5. Freelancing

Writing, design, virtual assistance, bookkeeping, video editing, web building. Start with clients who already know and trust you, then grow from referrals.

6. Content and community

Share your journey home on TikTok, YouTube or LinkedIn. Over time an audience can lead to your own products, brand partnerships and affiliate income. It's slower, so treat it as a second stream, not your first.

Your AI team: earning without hiring

You don't need staff or a big budget to start. AI tools can work like a small team: one helps you research what people are searching for, one drafts your product or service, one helps with design, one writes your ads and emails, and one handles admin. You stay the boss: you check everything, add your experience and make it sound like you.

The setup: internet, power and getting paid

  • Internet: have two connections, for example fibre or a home router plus a mobile data backup from a different network. Test them in the exact area you'll live in.
  • Power: plan for backup, such as solar with batteries, an inverter or a generator. Your laptop and router must stay on during outages.
  • Getting paid: keep at least one bank account abroad open, and check which payment platforms work in your country before you rely on them. For Nigerians, the Non-Resident BVN makes it possible to open naira and domiciliary (USD, GBP, EUR) accounts from abroad before you move.
  • Time zones: West Africa is about four to six hours ahead of the US East Coast, depending on the country and the time of year. Agree your working hours with clients in advance.
  • Tax: earning from one country while living in another can create tax obligations in both. Speak to a tax professional who understands both countries.

How to test your income before you move

  1. Start now, from where you live today. Build your product, land your first clients or agree remote work while you still have your salary.
  2. Run it for three months. Track what you actually earn, not what you hope to earn.
  3. Work from Africa during a trial stay. Spend a few weeks working from the city you'd live in. Test internet, power and time zones in real life.
  4. Decide with numbers. If the income held up during the trial, you're much closer to ready.

Common questions

How can I earn money after moving back to Africa?
The most common ways are keeping your job remotely, selling digital products, offering digital marketing services, coaching or consulting in your profession, freelancing, and building content and community. Start with one or two.
Should I move before I have income in Africa?
It is safer to have at least one income stream that already works from Africa before you buy a one-way ticket. Savings buy you time; income lets you stay.
Can I work remotely from Africa?
Yes, if your work allows it. You need two internet connections, backup power, a payment setup that works in your country, agreed working hours across time zones, and advice on tax in both countries.
How do I test my income before moving?
Start earning from where you live now, track real income for three months, then work from your chosen city during a trial stay of a few weeks to test internet, power and time zones.
